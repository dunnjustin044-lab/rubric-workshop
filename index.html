01 · CRITERIAAbout 1 minute

What will you assess?

Choose one important aspect of student work. A criterion names the evidence you value, not a task step or a personality trait.

Worked example · Lesson Plan Development

Assessment task: develop a 45-minute lesson plan aligned to outcomes, activities, learner needs and assessment.

Existing criteria in the example:

Learning outcomes & alignmentTeaching & learning activitiesInclusion & learner diversityAssessment strategies
Tip: Ask, “What quality in the submitted work matters most?”
02 · PERFORMANCE LEVELSAbout 1 minute

How will you distinguish quality?

Use four levels that are easy to understand and ordered from strongest to least evidence. You can rename them to fit your context.

ExemplaryConsistently strong evidence
ProficientEffective evidence overall
DevelopingPartial or uneven evidence
NoviceLimited or missing evidence
Keep the levels parallel: each should describe the same criterion, with a clear difference in quality or extent.
03 · PERFORMANCE DESCRIPTORSAbout 2 minutes

Show what each level looks like.

Use observable evidence. Make the differences specific enough that two markers could reach a similar judgement.

Worked example · criterion: Learning outcomes & alignment

Vague: “Good alignment.”

Clearer: “Outcomes are measurable and each is explicitly practised in a lesson activity and checked by an aligned assessment.”

Descriptor check: Can a marker point to evidence in the student’s work? Have you avoided words like “good” without explaining what they mean?
04 · SCORES & WEIGHTINGAbout 2 minutes

Make the score match the importance.

Choose the total mark for this criterion, then divide it across levels. The example used four criteria worth 25 marks each (100 total).
